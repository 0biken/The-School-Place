# The School Place
## Brand Guidelines v1.0

---

## 1. Brand Foundation

### 1.1 Brand Positioning
The School Place is a premium, personalized A-Level coaching service led by experienced educators and Cambridge assessors. It sits at the intersection of **academic rigor** and **individual care** — this is not a mass-market tutoring app, it's a bespoke, high-trust relationship between student and expert.

**Positioning statement:**
*For A-Level students who want to master their subjects and secure top university offers, The School Place provides personalized coaching from real Cambridge assessors — combining exam-board insider knowledge with one-on-one mentorship.*

### 1.2 Brand Pillars
1. **Expertise** — Real Cambridge assessors, not generalist tutors
2. **Personalization** — One-on-one, tailored to the individual student
3. **Results** — Exam-focused, measurable grade improvement
4. **Trust** — Calm, credible, parent-and-student-reassuring

### 1.3 Brand Voice
| Trait | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Authoritative | "Insight from examiners who set the papers" | "We're the BEST tutors ever!!" |
| Warm | "We'll work at your pace" | Cold, corporate jargon |
| Precise | Specific, exam-board-accurate language | Vague claims ("guaranteed A*") |
| Confident, not hype-y | Calm certainty | Excessive exclamation points, ALL CAPS |

### 1.4 Tagline options
- *Learn Smarter. Perform Better. Achieve Excellence.* (existing — keep, it's solid)
- Alt: *Examiner-Led. Student-Focused.*

## 2. Logo

### 2.1 Concept direction
Retire the stock graduation-cap clip-art mark. Replace with a **custom monogram** built from "SP" or "TSP," constructed with a geometric/architectural quality (think: the precision of a mathematical instrument — a set square, a compass arc, a protractor curve) rather than a literal school icon. This ties back to the "Mathematics/Physics/precision" subject matter and differentiates from generic education logos (caps, books, apples, owls).

**Alternative direction:** An abstract mark built from a single continuous line forming both a "checkmark" (achievement) and an ascending trend line (grade improvement) — reinforcing the "excellence" and "results" pillars visually.

### 2.2 Logo construction
- Primary lockup: Mark + wordmark, horizontal
- Secondary lockup: Mark + wordmark, stacked (for square social profile use)
- Icon-only mark: for favicons, WhatsApp DP, watermarks — must be legible at 32x32px

### 2.3 Clear space & minimum size
- Clear space = height of the "S" monogram on all sides
- Minimum digital size: 24px height
- Minimum print size: 15mm height

### 2.4 Logo misuse (prohibited)
- Don't recolor outside approved palette
- Don't stretch/distort proportions
- Don't place on busy photographic backgrounds without a solid contrast panel
- Don't recreate the wordmark in a different typeface

## 3. Color System

Move from decorative, interchangeable color use to a **functional system** — every color has one job.

### 3.1 Primary palette
| Role | Color | Hex (suggested) | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary — Deep Navy | Authority, trust | `#0B2A5B` | Headlines, logo, primary backgrounds |
| Accent — Academic Gold | Achievement, CTA | `#F2B705` | Buttons, highlights, "book now" moments ONLY |
| Neutral — Off-White | Breathing room | `#F7F7F5` | Backgrounds, negative space |
| Support — Slate Blue | Secondary info | `#4A6FA5` | Icons, subheads, secondary UI |

**Rule:** Gold is reserved exclusively for calls-to-action and key achievement moments (grades, results, "book your consultation"). If gold is used everywhere, it stops meaning "act now." Discipline here is what will make the brand feel premium instead of generic.

### 3.2 Color ratio guide
Roughly 60% neutral/white space, 30% navy, 10% gold accent. Currently the flyer over-uses gold and light blue decoratively — pull this back hard.

## 4. Typography

### 4.1 Type pairing
- **Display/Headline typeface:** A distinctive serif or semi-serif (e.g., in the spirit of *Fraunces*, *Lora*, or *Tiempos*) — signals academic gravitas and stands apart from the generic rounded-sans look every competitor uses.
- **Body/UI typeface:** A clean, highly legible grotesque sans (e.g., *Inter*, *Söhne*, *Public Sans*) for body copy, lists, and interface text.

### 4.2 Hierarchy
| Level | Typeface | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Display serif | Bold | Hero headlines only |
| H2 | Display serif | Medium | Section headers |
| Body | Sans | Regular | Paragraph copy |
| Labels/Captions | Sans | Medium, uppercase, tracked out | Icon labels, tags |

**Rule:** Never use more than two typefaces. Never let the body sans carry a headline — headlines always get the display serif, to keep a distinct "voice" between statement and explanation.

---

## 5. Iconography

Replace the current mismatched icon set (different stroke weights/fill styles per subject) with **one unified custom icon set**:
- Consistent stroke weight (e.g., 1.5px)
- Consistent corner radius
- Single style: either all outline or all filled — not mixed
- Icons drawn or commissioned specifically for: Physics, Mathematics, Chemistry, Biology, IT, plus supporting icons (calendar, phone, email, checkmark, clock)

This single change will do more for perceived professionalism than almost anything else on the list.

---

## 6. Imagery

- **Prioritize real photography** of tutors/sessions over illustration where possible — this is a trust-driven, relationship-based service, and a real face outperforms clip-art for credibility.
- If illustration is used, commission one custom illustration style (line weight, color fills matching the palette) rather than mixing stock graphics.
- Avoid generic "laptop + stacked books + graduation cap" stock scenes — overused across the tutoring/e-learning category.

---

## 7. Layout & Grid Principles

- Move from **bordered-box-per-section** layouts to a **whitespace-and-alignment-driven grid**. Hierarchy should come from spacing and type scale, not boxes and outlines.
- Establish a consistent margin/gutter system (e.g., 24px base unit) across all materials.
- Limit each composition to one primary focal point — currently the flyer has ~6 competing sections of equal visual weight.
